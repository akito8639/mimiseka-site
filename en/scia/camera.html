Camera

A proper camera, too.

An app for stories, so the photography comes second — no. It is built to be the one camera you use every day, where pressing the button is enough to get a good picture.

Taking a good one

No difficult settings are needed. But when you want to take care over it, you can.

Open the ⋯ at the top and six things sit together: flash, portrait, filter, timer, recording and location.

Portrait

Softens the background. The strength of the blur can still be adjusted after the shot, and that version is the one that gets saved and goes into the story.

Colour

Six tones to match the light of the day. You can choose again on the confirmation screen after the shot. A line sweeps across the seam, so you can see that it has changed.

Focus and brightness

Tap the screen to focus, and slide a finger to set the brightness. A long press locks the focus and the exposure where they are.

Zoom and flash

The zoom is stepless, with a slight catch under the finger at 1×, so it is easy to come back. The rear flash is the lamp; the front uses the screen itself as the light.

Taking one easily

So that nothing gets in the way at the moment you press.

The volume button as a shutter

The iPhone’s volume button takes the photo, so you need not be looking at the screen. On days with gloves, and on days too bright to see.

A timer that counts aloud

The five-second timer counts “3, 2, 1” in a voice, with a beat under your finger each second. When you want to be in the picture, you know the moment without watching the screen.

Held sideways

Turn the phone and the buttons and labels turn with it. For when a landscape wants to be wide.

Straight from the lock screen

A button in Control Centre or on the lock screen opens the camera at once, so the moment you wanted is not lost.

Keeping it

Something of the day stays with the photograph.

Place and weather

The name of the place you were in (down to a temple or a building), and the sky that day. Later, when a story gets written, these go straight into it.

Adding a voice

You can record a few words as you take the photo. A child’s voice, the sound of waves, how it felt just then — kept alongside the picture.

Bringing one in later

Photos already in your camera roll can be brought in afterwards, and the sky from the day they were taken comes with them.

And every photo you take gains a description, in a voice, written into the picture itself.
So that you know which one it is without looking, and inside the Photos app too.
