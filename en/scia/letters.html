Letters arrive.

One a day, from the you of another day. One a month, from Scia.
And the description written into each photo gets a choice of tone.

A letter for today

What were you writing, this day last year?
——Do you remember?

From the you of another day, one a day.

Every evening at ten, one envelope arrives on the desk under Journaling on the home screen. The sender is the you of another day.

What you wrote on this day a year ago. What you were thinking at the same place, or under the same sky. A conversation that stopped halfway, that day. The words you left come back on a sheet of paper, with nothing summarised and nothing rephrased, in the way you actually speak. So as you read, you hear your own voice.

Delivered 7 October To you,
Home for the first time in a while. Tea with my mother on the veranda. We weren't talking about anything in particular, and still, we both laughed about when to bring the laundry in. It was only that kind of evening, but I think it was enough. From you, a year ago

Break the seal, and read. Or listen. If the talking had stopped, you can pick it up from there.

Each letter lasts only its day. Leave it unopened and, by the next evening, the next one has taken its place. Rather than piling them up and counting them, we would like today's letter read today.

On the days you wrote a diary, you were, without knowing it, writing a letter to a future you.

Letters begin once you have three chapters or so. Until then, Scia's first letter sits on the desk. No plan is required.

A letter for the month

What kind of month was last month?
——Could you say, in a word?

From Scia, one a month.

When the month turns, Last month in review arrives on the home screen. At the top of it sits a letter from Scia.

Everything you said about your photos over the month, everything you wrote down: Scia remembers it, as the friend who was sitting beside you, listening. And now that the month has turned, Scia thinks back and speaks to you. “That day on the veranda, you were laughing.” A day you had forgotten comes back in a person's voice.

On your own, you cannot see a month as one continuous thing. The days scatter as soon as they pass. Only when someone gathers them up and hands them back in a single line can you think, “so that is the month it was.”

The letter can be heard in Scia's voice: the voice Scia uses when speaking to you, separate from the story and guidance voices.

On the same page are the photos you kept coming back to last month, and one photo from the same month a year ago. Bind these into a book turns that line of photos into a book, as it is.

With a paid plan it arrives every month. On the free plan it arrives for the first three months. The notification comes once, at the start of the month.
Bind the photos into a book, and the letter for the month becomes that book's foreword. It stays on the cover, so every time you open the book later, it begins with that month's letter.

Two tones for the description

You want to tell them, and the words will not come.
——Has that ever happened to you?

Polite, or friendly. You choose the tone of the description written into each photo.

Friendly was born from one voice: someone from abroad, living in Japan. They wanted to tell a Japanese friend, or their own child, what they were looking at and what they felt about it. But the words would not come. If they could take a photo and have a description written, they could simply show it, or read it aloud. It would reach the other person, and it would be Japanese practice for themselves. Hearing that, we prepared a friendly, spoken Japanese: the way a teacher talks to young children.

The description written into each photo has only ever used polite, formal Japanese. From the next update you can choose Friendly.

Polite白いマグカップが、窓からの光を受けています。奥には観葉植物があります。
Friendly白いマグカップがあるよ。窓から光が入ってるよ。奥には、みどりの葉っぱがあるね。

Switch it from the Description pill on the camera screen. Below the length (brief, standard, detailed) sit the two tones (polite, friendly). The Description row of the style sheet offers the same choice. If you change nothing, it stays polite, as before.

A friendly description can have a voice of its own. When the description voice is an AI voice, a row called Friendly description voice appears under “Description voice” in Settings. Pick a voice that suits the tone, and the reading right after the shot, and the chapter's reading later, both use it.

The tone applies only to the description written into the photo. The wording of diaries and stories does not change. Descriptions are written in Japanese, so the tone applies to that Japanese text.
